# Exercise 1: Benefits and boundaries of Power Automate

<ExerciseModuleHeader :module="1" />

<CourseProgress :current="1" />

Use an internal training-request example to distinguish repeatable rules from decisions that need human judgement. Identify questions a leader should ask before supporting automation.

**Time:** 09:30–10:10 and 10:45–11:05, 60 minutes total.

> **Licence:** This paper activity requires no paid software licence or Power Automate account. You will not build a flow.

## Preparation

- Work in groups of 4–6. Choose a recorder, timekeeper, and spokesperson.
- Each group needs two flipchart sheets, one blank A4 sheet, six sticky notes marked A–F, five sticky notes per participant, and pens or pencils.
- The facilitator will project and read the fictional case, activity prompts, and knowledge-check questions. You do not need a computer, account, download, or opened file.
- Keep both flipcharts and the A4 sheet for the next exercise.

> **Optional reference after the activity:** The [case pack](/resources/case-pack) and [worksheets](/resources/worksheets) repeat the fictional information and recording formats. They are not required during the live activity.

## Practice 1: Compare before and after (20 minutes) {#practice-1}

**Primary target:** Explain how one part of the work could change when a system follows agreed rules.

**Learner output:** One flipchart before/after canvas with four completed areas and one expected benefit.

1. Listen while the facilitator presents the fictional process from request submission to notification of the decision.
2. Divide the first flipchart sheet into four areas. Label them **Current repeated step**, **System could support**, **Human decision**, and **Check before trial**. Leave space at the bottom for **Expected benefit**.
3. Each participant writes one repeated copying or follow-up step on a sticky note and places it in **Current repeated step**.
4. Agree on one repeated step to explore. Keep that sticky note on the canvas and set the others aside.
5. Write one part the system could support under agreed rules on a sticky note and place it in **System could support**.
6. Write the decision that remains with the manager on a sticky note and place it in **Human decision**.
7. Write one output, permission, connection, or rule that must be checked before a trial. Name the responsible role and place the sticky note in **Check before trial**.
8. At the bottom, add one **Expected benefit**. Do not present it as a measured result.

### Checkpoint

The flipchart canvas identifies a specific change, a human decision owner, a check before trial, and one benefit labelled **Expected**.

## Practice 2: Sort scenario cards (20 minutes) {#practice-2}

**Primary target:** Classify scenarios by the role of automation and the information still needed.

**Learner output:** One flipchart sorting board with all six scenario sticky notes, reasons, and verification owners where needed.

1. Divide the second flipchart into three columns: **System can help under agreed rules**, **Human decision needed**, and **More information needed**.
2. Write A, B, C, D, E, and F on six separate sticky notes.
3. Use this guide for each scenario:
   1. If a rule, access, or responsible person is unknown, choose **More information needed**.
   2. Otherwise, if someone must judge or approve, choose **Human decision needed**.
   3. Otherwise, when the rule and access are clear, choose **System can help under agreed rules**.
4. Listen while the facilitator projects and reads scenarios A–C. Place each matching sticky note in one column.
5. Repeat for scenarios D–F.
6. Add a short reason beside every sticky note. For **More information needed**, add the missing information and the role that should verify it.
7. Select one disputed card to discuss during Q&A at 10:10–10:25.
8. Keep the sorting board visible for the knowledge check after the break.

### Scenario cards

**A.** A request arrives in the approved mailbox. Notify the coordinator.

**B.** A request does not match an agreed rule. A manager must decide whether it is suitable.

**C.** The date is blank. The agreed rule says to ask the requester for it.

**D.** Copy data from another system. Access has not been confirmed.

**E.** At the agreed time, remind the agreed person about a waiting request.

**F.** The manager has not decided. Tell the requester it is approved.

### Checkpoint

All six sticky notes have a classification and reason. Every unresolved question has a verification owner. Sending an approval request is not treated as making the approval decision.

## Practice 3: Knowledge check (20 minutes) {#practice-3}

**Primary target:** Demonstrate an accurate understanding of benefits and limits through reasoned answers.

**Learner output:** Three revised answer sticky notes per participant and one group A4 record of a known fact, open question, and verification owner.

1. Take three sticky notes and number them 1, 2, and 3.
2. Answer the first projected question on sticky note 1: what is one task in your work that happens the same way every time?
3. Answer the second question on sticky note 2: which part of a work process still needs human judgement, and why?
4. Answer the third question on sticky note 3: what information would you check before automating a task?
5. Compare all three answers with a partner. Rewrite or amend any answer whose reasoning has changed.
6. Divide the A4 sheet into **Known fact**, **Open question**, and **Verification owner**. Agree one entry for each area after the facilitator's explanation.

### Checkpoint

Every participant has three reasoned answers. The group A4 sheet contains one supported fact and one open question with a named verification owner.

## If you get stuck

Ask the facilitator to repeat the projected case or scenario. Mark unknown rules or permissions **Needs verification**, with the role responsible for answering. Do not invent an answer for the process owner.

## Summary

You have identified work a system could support and decisions people retain. Use this distinction when mapping friction in Exercise 2.
